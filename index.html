About me

Music has always been part of my life.

My journey with the harp began at age nine — a moment of quiet fascination that grew into a lifelong calling. Over the years, the harp has taken me from the practice room to concert halls, cathedrals, gardens and events across Malta.

In 2025, I received my Diploma in Harp Performance from the Malta School of Music, studying under Ms Caroline Muscat. I also hold a Diploma in Violin Performance, giving me experience performing from both sides of an ensemble.

I currently perform with the Malta Youth Orchestra as well as at weddings, private celebrations and corporate events. I play both pedal and Celtic harp, and enjoy creating programmes that combine classical music with familiar contemporary pieces.

For each event, I work with my clients to choose music that suits the setting — whether that means carefully planning a wedding ceremony or simply creating the right atmosphere while guests arrive and mingle.

FAQs

Good to know.

How far in advance should I enquire?

As early as you can, especially for weddings and for the busy summer and festive seasons. Last-minute enquiries are always welcome too — send the details and I will let you know whether I am free.

Can I request specific songs?

Absolutely. We can build the programme together, from the processional to the last dance of the evening, mixing classical, pop and film music.

Which harp do you play?

I play both pedal and Celtic harp, and will recommend the best one for your venue and the style of music.

Do you perform with other musicians?

Yes. As well as solo harp, I perform as a harp and cello duo and can collaborate with singers and other instrumentalists.

How do I secure my date?

Send me an enquiry using the form below. I will reply with availability and options, and we can confirm the details from there.

Beyond music

A second side of my work.

Alongside my work as a harpist, I am a mechanical engineer and researcher at the University of Malta. My undergraduate research brought my two fields together through the development of a prosthetic-foot concept for pedal harpists, informed by biomechanical analysis of harp-pedal movement.

The work was subsequently presented and published at an international engineering conference.
